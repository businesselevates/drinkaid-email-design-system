THE EVIDENCE
Tested as sold,
not as ingredients.
Hi {{ first_name|default:'there' }},

You've probably seen supplements described as “science-backed” or “clinically tested.”

Sometimes, that means one or two ingredients in the formula have been studied.

That's useful. But it doesn't tell you what happens when you put all the ingredients together in the finished product.

We wanted to know that too. So we took DrinkAid to NUS and Temasek Polytechnic and tested the formula and its key ingredients.
1
NUS tested the ingredients
NUS tested four key DrinkAid ingredients. Three of them — DHM, Pyroglutamic Acid and S-Acetyl Glutathione — showed a statistically significant increase of roughly 2× in ALDH2 gene expression after 24 hours. ALDH2 is one of the enzymes involved in breaking down acetaldehyde, the toxic byproduct produced when your body processes alcohol.
2
Temasek Polytechnic tested the finished formula
This is where it gets more interesting. Temasek Polytechnic tested the complete DrinkAid formula, rather than looking at individual ingredients in isolation. The study found that DrinkAid increased ALDH2 activity, increased glutathione levels by around 2.6×, and reduced markers associated with liver stress.
So we have two different pieces of evidence. NUS looked at what key ingredients can do. Temasek Polytechnic tested what the finished formula does as a whole. That's the distinction we care about.

And while we're talking about formulation, we also chose to use S-Acetyl Glutathione rather than cheaper glutathione alternatives. It's significantly more expensive, but we chose it because of its bioavailability and role in the formula.

Nobody sees that decision on the front of the box. We made it anyway.
